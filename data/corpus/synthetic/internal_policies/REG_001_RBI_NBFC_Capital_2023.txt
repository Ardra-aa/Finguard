REGULATORY — RESERVE BANK OF INDIA — MASTER DIRECTION

**RBI Master Direction — Non-Banking Financial Companies — Capital Adequacy Norms**

**Document ID:** RBI-NBFC-MASTER-2023
**Regulation Reference:** RBI/DNBR/2023-24/MD-01
**Effective Date:** October 19, 2023
**Jurisdiction:** India (IN)
**Regulatory Scope:** Banking / Non-Banking Financial Companies
**Risk Level:** HIGH

---

**1. Preamble**

In exercise of the powers conferred by Sections 45-IA, 45JA, 45K, 45L, and 45MA of the Reserve Bank of India Act, 1934, the Reserve Bank of India hereby issues this Master Direction on Capital Adequacy Norms for Non-Banking Financial Companies (NBFCs). This Direction consolidates and supersedes all previous circulars and guidelines on NBFC capital adequacy issued prior to October 19, 2023.

**2. Applicability**

This Master Direction applies to all Non-Banking Financial Companies registered with the Reserve Bank of India, including:
- NBFC-Investment and Credit Companies (NBFC-ICC)
- NBFC-Micro Finance Institutions (NBFC-MFI)
- NBFC-Factors
- Infrastructure Finance Companies (NBFC-IFC)
- Core Investment Companies (CIC)
- Housing Finance Companies (HFC) to the extent applicable

**3. Minimum Capital to Risk-Weighted Assets Ratio (CRAR)**

3.1. Every applicable NBFC shall maintain a minimum Capital to Risk-Weighted Assets Ratio (CRAR) of 15% (fifteen percent) with effect from October 19, 2023.

3.2. Of the above, the minimum Tier I Capital ratio shall be 10% (ten percent) of the total risk-weighted assets.

3.3. NBFCs classified under the Upper Layer shall maintain a minimum CRAR of 15% with Common Equity Tier 1 (CET1) capital of at least 9% of risk-weighted assets.

3.4. The leverage ratio for Upper Layer NBFCs shall not exceed 25 times of Tier I capital.

**4. Tier I Capital Requirements**

4.1. Tier I Capital (Core Capital) shall consist of:
(a) Paid-up equity capital
(b) Free reserves including share premium
(c) Capital reserves representing surplus arising out of sale proceeds of assets
(d) Statutory reserves
(e) Balance in the Profit and Loss account after appropriation towards dividend, bonus shares, etc.

4.2. The following shall be deducted from Tier I Capital:
(a) Intangible assets
(b) Deferred Tax Assets (DTA) exceeding 10% of CET1
(c) Investments in subsidiaries and associates in the nature of equity
(d) Accumulated losses

**5. Tier II Capital Components**

5.1. Tier II Capital (Supplementary Capital) shall consist of:
(a) Revaluation reserves (discounted at 55%)
(b) General provisions and loss reserves up to 1.25% of total risk-weighted assets
(c) Hybrid capital instruments
(d) Subordinated debt with a minimum original maturity of 5 years (discounted by 20% each year during the last 5 years)

5.2. The total of Tier II Capital elements shall be limited to a maximum of 100% of the total of Tier I Capital.

**6. Risk-Weighted Assets Computation**

6.1. Credit Risk: Assets shall be risk-weighted based on counterparty classification:
- Cash and bank balances: 0%
- Government securities: 0%
- Approved securities: 20%
- Housing loans up to INR 35 lakhs: 35%
- Housing loans above INR 35 lakhs: 50-75%
- Standard asset loans to corporates: 100%
- Non-performing assets (sub-standard): 150%
- Non-performing assets (doubtful): 200%

6.2. Off-balance sheet items shall be converted to credit risk equivalents using Credit Conversion Factors (CCF) as specified.

**7. Capital Conservation Buffer**

7.1. NBFCs in the Upper Layer shall maintain a Capital Conservation Buffer (CCB) of 2.5% of risk-weighted assets, comprised entirely of Common Equity Tier 1 capital.

7.2. The CCB shall be phased in as follows:
- From April 1, 2024: 0.625%
- From April 1, 2025: 1.25%
- From April 1, 2026: 1.875%
- From April 1, 2027: 2.5%

**8. Internal Capital Adequacy Assessment Process (ICAAP)**

8.1. Every NBFC classified in the Middle Layer or Upper Layer shall have an ICAAP to assess the adequacy of its capital vis-à-vis its risk profile.

8.2. The ICAAP shall cover all material risks not captured under Pillar 1, including concentration risk, interest rate risk in the banking book, liquidity risk, strategic risk, and reputational risk.

**9. Reporting Requirements**

9.1. NBFCs shall submit quarterly returns on capital adequacy to the Reserve Bank within 15 days from the end of each quarter.

9.2. The Board of Directors shall review the CRAR position at least quarterly.

**10. Penalties for Non-Compliance**

10.1. NBFCs failing to maintain the prescribed CRAR shall be subject to supervisory action including restrictions on expansion of credit, dividend declaration, and opening of new branches.
